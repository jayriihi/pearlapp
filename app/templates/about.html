About the Stations

Pearl Island Station

Pearl Island is the ideal location for measuring the wind, with unobstructed exposure in all directions in the Great Sound, where much of Bermuda’s wind sports activity takes place. The station operates with the kind permission of the Bermuda Audubon Society.

The station is built around a Gill WindSonic 75 ultrasonic anemometer, a state-of-the-art sensor used in airports and marine facilities around the globe. It delivers wind-speed accuracy to within 0.5 knots and is rated for wind speeds from 0 to 145 knots.

Crescent Station

An autonomous weather station and AIS beacon, managed by Bermuda Maritime Operations Centre, Department of Marine and Ports Services. It is securely affixed to a channel marker on the North Channel, approximately 4 miles north of Dockyard. This location marks a critical point where the channel approach follows a graceful "crescent" shaped bend, leading into a chute in the reef measuring about 200 meters wide.

Imagine piloting a cruise ship, 330 meters long and 47 meters wide, with an exposed windage area of approximately 25,000 square meters. At this precise bend, knowing the current wind conditions becomes indispensable!

NMB Station

National Museum Bermuda is a station run by the Bermuda Weather Service on the roof of Commissioner's House at the tip of Dockyard.
